# Chlorine Oxidation Kinetics in Simulated Swimming Pool Water

**Independent Experimental Research | IB Chemistry Internal Assessment**

## Research Overview

This project investigates how pH affects chlorine oxidation kinetics in simulated swimming pool water, using the oxidation of indigo carmine dye as an experimental indicator.

The research was motivated by a practical question encountered during swimming pool maintenance: why must pH be carefully controlled to maintain chlorine effectiveness?

## Experimental Methodology

- Prepared simulated swimming pool water with controlled chemical composition.
- Investigated seven pH conditions ranging from 6.0 to 9.0.
- Used a PASCO wireless colorimeter to monitor changes in dye absorbance over time.
- Applied the Beer–Lambert law to interpret absorbance as an indicator of dye concentration.
- Used linear regression to estimate and compare absorbance-change rates under different pH conditions.

## Key Findings

- The highest measured oxidation rates occurred within the approximately neutral pH range of 7.0–8.0.
- Oxidation behaviour at more extreme pH values deviated from the initial hypothesis.
- Linear models showed weaker goodness of fit under extreme conditions, highlighting limitations in the reliability of the estimated rates.
- The investigation explored potential chemical explanations for these deviations, including changes in chlorine speciation and possible chlorine loss under acidic conditions.

## Technical Skills Demonstrated

**Experimental Design:** Controlled variables, solution preparation, sensor calibration, laboratory procedures

**Quantitative Analysis:** Reaction kinetics, absorbance measurements, linear regression, model evaluation

**Scientific Reasoning:** Hypothesis testing, interpretation of unexpected results, uncertainty and experimental limitations

## Limitations

This was an educational laboratory investigation using simulated swimming pool water. Reaction rates were estimated from changes in dye absorbance rather than direct measurement of individual chlorine species. Measurement delays, mixing effects, and weaker model fits under extreme pH conditions limit the precision of some estimates.
